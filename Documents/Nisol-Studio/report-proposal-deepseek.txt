The author has correctly identified a critical strategic flaw: **mixing the "Discovery" deliverable with the "Implementation Sales" pitch.** 

Putting a fixed fee ($120k) and payment milestones inside a *Discovery Report* does the following:

1. **Dilutes your authority**: It makes the report look like a sales brochure rather than an objective strategic assessment.
2. **Creates friction**: The client isn't ready to sign a $120k check for implementation while they are still digesting a 10-page readiness report.
3. **Confuses the timeline**: They just finished paying for Discovery. Throwing a rigid "In/Out of Scope" table at them now feels premature and defensive.

The file's recommendation to pivot to **"Recommended Implementation Roadmap & Investment"** with **tiered engagement options (Quick Wins / Department / Enterprise)** is precisely how KPMG and Deloitte structure their "Path to Value" sections.

### Why This Implementation Wins

1. **Answers "What should we build first?"** (Section 7.1) – The priority table immediately tells the client which use case has the highest business value and shortest timeline.
2. **Provides budget optionality** (Section 7.2) – Instead of a single scary number, it shows ranges per blueprint, reducing sticker shock.
3. **Sells a Journey, not a Project** (Section 7.3) – The 3-tier model (Quick Wins, Department, Enterprise) lets the client self-identify their risk appetite and budget. This is the exact "Path to Value" framework used by Deloitte.
4. **Moves the "Hard Close" to a separate document** (Section 7.4) – By explicitly stating that a *fixed-price commercial proposal* will follow *after* they select an option, you remove the pressure from the discovery report. This makes you look consultative, not transactional.
