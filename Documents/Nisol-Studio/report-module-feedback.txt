# My Biggest Recommendation

I would **stop thinking in terms of "10 AI outputs."**

Instead think in terms of **report sections**.

For example:

```
Report

├── Executive Dashboard
│     ├── KPI Cards
│     ├── Spider Chart
│     ├── Heat Map
│
├── Executive Summary
│
├── Current State
│
├── AI Readiness
│
├── Department Analysis
│
├── Opportunity Portfolio
│
├── ROI Analysis
│
├── Transformation Roadmap
│
├── Governance Assessment
│
├── Data Readiness
│
├── Solution Blueprints
│
└── Proposal
```

Each section owns:

* Its own data source
* Its own calculations
* Its own visualization
* Its own AI narrative
* Its own rendering template

## Overall verdict

**Current implementation:** **8.8/10**

It is a solid, production-worthy MVP with good error handling, fallback mechanisms, and a sensible generation pipeline. 

**Target for Nisol AI:** **10/10**

To reach that level, I would shift from an **AI-first report generator** to a **rules-driven report engine** where:

* **Software** computes scores, rankings, ROI, prioritization, roadmaps, and chart data.
* **The LLM** explains the results in executive language.
* **A template engine** assembles reusable sections into PDF, web, or PowerPoint outputs.

That architecture will produce more consistent reports, reduce LLM costs, improve testability, and give Nisol AI a report quality that feels like a mature consulting product rather than an AI-generated document.
